==============================================================================
ClusterWorx — Voltage Divider / Zener / Optocoupler Notes
==============================================================================
Context: All Arduino input pins (analog AV2-AV13 and digital DV*) currently
use only a bare 10kOhm series resistor, no voltage divider, relying on the
Arduino's internal ESD/clamp diodes to survive 12-14V automotive signals.
Vehicle is a 12V system, alternator charging up to ~14.4V, load-dump
transients can briefly exceed that significantly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THE CURRENT 10kOhm-ONLY SETUP IS RISKY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- At 12V: clamp current = (12 - 5.3) / 10k =~ 0.7mA
- At 14.4V: clamp current =~ 0.9mA
  (right at the ~1mA recommended max continuous clamp current per pin)
- Across ~20 pins simultaneously during a load-dump spike, this is a real
  risk to the MCU.
- Analog pins: anything above ~5V just clamps flat -> no usable resolution
  above 5V, only good for crude ON/OFF detection, not real voltage readings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CHEAPEST RETROFIT — ADD ONE RESISTOR TO GND AT THE ARDUINO END
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No harness rewiring needed — just add a second resistor from each Arduino
pin to GND, turning the existing 10kOhm series resistor into a real divider.

  ANALOG (voltage-sense) pins, e.g. AV2-AV13:
    Add 5.1kOhm to GND  -> ratio =~ 0.337 (close to /3)
      12V   -> ~4.0V at ADC
      14.4V -> ~4.85V at ADC (still under 5V)
    Update VOLTAGE_SCALE to ~2.96 (1/0.337) to recover real volts in code.

  DIGITAL (ON/OFF) pins, e.g. ignition, standby, glow plug, indicators,
  seatbelt, parking brake:
    Add 4.7kOhm to GND -> ratio =~ 0.32
      12V   -> ~3.8V  (clean HIGH on 5V logic)
      14.4V -> ~4.6V  (still under 5V)
    Removes reliance on internal clamp diodes for continuous operation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ZENER PROTECTION FOR ANALOG (AV) PINS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use a 5.1V Zener (e.g. BZX55C5V1 / 1N5231B), wired:
    cathode -> ADC pin
    anode   -> GND
Placed AFTER the divider (i.e. on the Arduino-side node, not before).

Why 5.1V:
  - Stays OFF during normal operation (divider output maxes ~4.8-4.85V)
  - Clamps just above the 5V rail but below ATmega absolute max
    (Vcc + 0.5V =~ 5.5V)
  - Protects ADC input during load-dump transients without nuisance
    clamping in normal driving.

Power rating: irrelevant here, current is sub-mA. A small SOT-23/SOD-80
SMD part (BZX84C5V1) is fine.

Alternative: small Schottky diode (1N5819 / BAT54) from pin to the 5V rail
instead of GND — clamps overvoltage into the 5V supply, slightly faster.

Same 5.1V Zener works equally well on the digital pins too (one part number
for the whole retrofit).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DIGITAL PINS — SEATBELT & PARKING BRAKE SWITCHES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are simple switch-to-ground type signals riding on a ~12V line
(typically the warning-lamp feed): ~12V when switch open, ~0V when switch
closes to ground (or vice versa depending on wiring).

Same retrofit as other digital pins:
  - 4.7kOhm to GND (forms divider with existing 10kOhm series resistor)
      12V -> ~3.85V (clean HIGH), 0V -> 0V (clean LOW)
  - 5.1V Zener (BZX55C5V1) pin -> GND for transient protection

Note: mechanical switch contacts (especially seatbelt buckle switches) can
bounce / transition slowly compared to electronic signals. If the dashboard
icon flickers briefly on buckle/unbuckle, that's a SOFTWARE fix (debounce —
require new state to persist ~50ms before latching SeatbeltSeen /
ParkingBrakeSeen), not a wiring issue. No special hardware needed beyond the
standard divider + Zener.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. INDICATOR PINS — RESISTOR VALUE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Depends on what the V508VC regulator is actually outputting — CHECK WITH A
METER first (indicator stalk ON):

  CASE A — V508VC output swings full 12V (just buffered, not regulated down):
    Read close to 12V at the Arduino pin.
    -> Same as other digital pins: add 4.7kOhm to GND + 5.1V Zener to GND.
       12V pulse becomes ~3.85V at the pin.

  CASE B — V508VC is a true 5V regulator (output already ~5V when active):
    Read close to 5V at the Arduino pin.
    -> Do NOT add the 4.7kOhm divider (would needlessly drop signal to
       ~3.85V and reduce margin above HIGH threshold).
    -> Keep existing 10kOhm series resistor as-is.
    -> Add only the 5.1V Zener to GND for transient protection.

Either way, the 5.1V Zener is cheap insurance against switching-edge
overshoot from the flasher relay — add it regardless of which case applies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. OPTOCOUPLER NOTES (separate from the above — opto inputs, not direct
   Arduino pins)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A resistive divider placed BEFORE an optocoupler's input LED does NOT help
and can make things worse:
  - The opto LED clamps its own node voltage at its forward voltage
    (~1.2-1.4V) when conducting, same way a Zener does.
  - A divider resistor to ground bleeds off current that should be driving
    the LED -> LESS LED current for a given series resistor -> weaker,
    less reliable switching (slow/incomplete turn-on, marginal CTR,
    especially as the opto ages or in cold weather). This can look exactly
    like "the opto can't handle it" when really it's underdriven.

Likely real causes of optocoupler "strain" with a bare 10kOhm series
resistor:
  1. LED current too low for reliable switching:
       At 12V: I =~ (12 - 1.2) / 10k =~ 1.1mA
       Many PC817-class optos want 5-10mA for healthy CTR margin.
  2. Load-dump/transient spikes hitting the LED's REVERSE voltage rating
     (typically only ~5-6V for opto LEDs). Negative excursions (alternator
     ripple, relay back-EMF, switching noise) beyond that degrade or punch
     through the LED over time.
  3. Sustained over-current during transients (e.g. 30-40V spike through
     10kOhm still pushes several extra mA into the LED) -> slow opto
     degradation / eventual failure to trigger.

RECOMMENDED FIX (priority order):
  a) Resize the series resistor for ~8-10mA at nominal 12V:
       R =~ (12 - 1.2) / 0.009 =~ 1.2kOhm
     Gives a strong, reliable switching signal with good margin.
  b) Add a TVS or Zener clamp (15-18V) across the opto's input, AHEAD of
     the LED — stays off during normal 12-14V operation, clamps load-dump
     transients before they reach the LED.
  c) Add an anti-parallel diode across the LED (cathode-to-anode) to shunt
     reverse-polarity transients away from the LED's low reverse-voltage
     junction.

Bottom line: reserve resistive dividers for signals going DIRECTLY into
Arduino pins (no opto). For opto-coupled inputs, fix with (a) correct series
resistor for LED current, (b) TVS/Zener clamp ahead of the opto, and
(c) anti-parallel reverse-protection diode.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY SHOPPING LIST
==============================================================================
- Resistors: 4.7kOhm and 5.1kOhm (1/4W, for the divider retrofits)
- Resistors: 1.2kOhm (for re-biasing optocoupler LED inputs)
- Zener diodes: 5.1V (BZX55C5V1 / 1N5231B) — one per protected pin
- Zener/TVS: 15-18V — one per optocoupler input, for transient clamping
- Diodes: small signal (1N4148 or similar) — anti-parallel across each
  opto LED for reverse-voltage protection
